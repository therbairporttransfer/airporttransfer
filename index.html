Which London Airport Is Easiest to Reach? The Journey to Each One Compared

London has five main airports, in five different directions. The cheapest flight is not always the best one once you add the journey to the terminal.

By {{AUTHOR_NAME}} · {{DATE_PUBLISHED}}

The short answer: no London airport is easiest for everyone. It depends on where you start. London City is closest to the centre and the City. Heathrow has the most rail options from west London. Gatwick suits south London and the south coast, Luton suits north London and the Midlands, and Stansted suits east London, Essex and Cambridge. Compare the full door-to-terminal journey, at the time of day you will actually travel, before you book the flight.

Flight comparison sites treat "London" as one destination, but Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Luton and London City are spread across the whole south-east. A flight that is cheaper from one airport can easily cost more in time, fares and stress once you add the ground journey, especially with luggage, children or a very early departure.

Start with the map, not the fare

Each airport sits on a different side of London, and the rail links are built around that geography. As a rough guide, Heathrow lies about 14 miles west of central London, Gatwick about 30 miles south, Luton about 30 miles north, Stansted about 40 miles north-east, and London City a few miles east in the Royal Docks.

The practical test is simple: how many changes does it take to get from your front door, or your hotel, to the terminal? An airport 40 miles away with a direct train from your nearest station can be easier than one 14 miles away that needs three changes.

The journey to each airport

Heathrow

Heathrow has the widest choice of rail links. The Heathrow Express runs to Paddington in about 15 minutes, the Elizabeth line crosses central London to the City and Canary Wharf without a change, and the Piccadilly line serves west and central London more slowly. On Friday and Saturday nights, the Night Tube runs on the Piccadilly line. Heathrow suits west London, the Thames Valley and anywhere on the Elizabeth line.

Gatwick

Gatwick's station is at the South Terminal, with a free shuttle from the North Terminal. The Gatwick Express runs to Victoria twice an hour in about 30 minutes. Thameslink serves London Bridge, Blackfriars, Farringdon and St Pancras, and Southern covers Clapham Junction and much of the south-east. Gatwick suits south London, Surrey, Sussex and the south coast.

Stansted

The Stansted Express runs from below the terminal to Liverpool Street, calling at Tottenham Hale, in about 48 minutes. Coaches run to several London stops around the clock. Stansted suits north-east London, Essex, Hertfordshire and Cambridge, but it is a long way from west or south London.

Luton

Luton Airport Parkway is served by the East Midlands Railway Luton Airport Express from St Pancras, in as little as 32 minutes, and by Thameslink. The Luton DART shuttle links the station to the terminal in under four minutes. Luton suits north London, Bedfordshire and the Midland Main Line towns, but early-morning departures can leave before the first trains arrive.

London City

London City Airport has its own DLR station, with trains towards Bank in the City, Stratford International and Woolwich Arsenal. It is the closest airport to central London and Canary Wharf, which makes it popular with business travellers. Its route network is smaller than the larger airports', so it is not an option for every trip.

When door to door makes more sense

Rail links are excellent for one person with a small bag, going from a central station at a busy time of day. They are less helpful in four common situations:

  • You are starting outside London. A journey from Hertfordshire, Kent or the Thames Valley to an airport on the other side of London can involve two terminuses and a cross-London transfer.
  • You are flying at the edges of the day. First and last trains, weekend engineering works and the limited Night Tube all narrow your choices.
  • You are travelling as a family or group. Several fares can approach the cost of one shared vehicle, and keeping everyone together through stations takes effort.
  • You are carrying a lot. Ski bags, pushchairs and several large cases make stairs, lifts and crowded carriages hard work.

In these cases, it is worth comparing the train with pre-booked airport transfers to the airport you are flying from. Whichever operator you use, give your flight number, state your exact luggage, request child seats if needed, and confirm the pick-up or meeting point in advance.

The hidden cost of a cheaper flight

A fare difference only means something once you add the ground journey on both legs. Before choosing an airport for price, compare:

  1. Travel time door to terminal, at the hour you would actually travel.
  2. Fares for everyone travelling, both ways, including any express train premium.
  3. Departure and arrival times. A 6am departure from a distant airport may need a hotel night or a very early car.
  4. Luggage charges, which vary by airline and can cancel out a cheap fare.
  5. The return journey. A late arrival into an airport far from home is where many trips end badly.

Running these numbers often confirms the cheaper flight. Sometimes it shows that the airport an hour closer to home saves money overall.

If you need extra help getting through the airport

Passengers with reduced mobility, hidden disabilities or other access needs can ask for free assistance at UK airports. Under rules overseen by the Civil Aviation Authority, help is arranged through your airline, ideally at least 48 hours before you fly. Assistance usually starts at a designated arrival point at the airport, so the ground journey matters here too.

Look at how easily you can reach that point from the transport you plan to use: step-free access at the station, the distance from the coach bays, or a set-down area close to the terminal. For some travellers, that one practical detail decides the airport before price or flight times come into it.

Mistakes when choosing a London airport

  • Assuming every "London" airport is in London. Stansted and Luton are in different counties from the capital.
  • Comparing train times without the journey to the London terminus.
  • Forgetting that rail timetables change at weekends and on bank holidays.
  • Booking an early flight before checking whether any public transport runs at that hour.
  • Planning the outbound journey carefully and leaving the return to chance.

Train times and frequencies come from the airports and operators and can change. Check National Rail and TfL for your travel date.

Final thoughts

The "best" London airport is usually the one that is easiest to reach from where you start, at the time you travel. Look at the map, count the changes and include both legs of the journey before letting a fare difference make the decision.

Frequently asked questions

Which London airport is closest to central London?

London City Airport, in the Royal Docks east of the City, is the closest. Of the larger airports, Heathrow is the nearest, about 14 miles west.

Which London airport has the fastest train?

The Heathrow Express is quickest, at about 15 minutes to Paddington. The Gatwick Express takes about 30 minutes to Victoria, and the Luton Airport Express about 32 minutes from St Pancras plus the DART shuttle.

Are Stansted and Luton really London airports?

They serve London but sit outside it: Stansted is in Essex and Luton in Bedfordshire. Both have direct train links to central London termini.

Is it worth paying more to fly from a closer airport?

Often, yes, when you add up ground travel time, fares for everyone in your party and the timing of your flights. Compare the total cost door to door, not just the airfare.
